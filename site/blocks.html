Types

Stabilized or un-stabilized?

The single biggest decision after size. The short answer: if the wall will see rain, stabilize it.

Un-stabilized Pure earth

Natural earth only: clay, sand, aggregate and silt.

  • Lowest cost, nothing added
  • Goes on the wall straight from the machine, no curing
  • Ideal clay content: 20–35%
  • Must be protected from weather by deep porch roofs, or a lime, earthen, stucco or paint finish

Stabilized ~8% cement or lime

About 92% earth and 8% cement, lime or fly ash.

  • Fully water resistant
  • As strong as, and denser than, traditional masonry
  • Ideal clay content: 15–25%, with more sand (cement bonds with sand)
  • Needs to cure before laying. Works anywhere other masonry does, including basements

Common approach: stabilized blocks on the exterior, un-stabilized inside. AECT's Building Off The Grid home used 6,500 stabilized exterior blocks and 3,000 un-stabilized interior blocks.

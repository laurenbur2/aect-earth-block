How it works

From dirt to walls in four steps

  1. Step 1

    Dig

    Use the soil on your own land, about 12" below the topsoil.

  2. Step 2

    Press

    An AECT machine compresses it into dense, uniform blocks, 300 to 480 an hour.

  3. Step 3

    Stack & cure

    Palletize the blocks. Pure-earth blocks can go straight onto the wall.

  4. Step 4

    Build

    Lay them like any masonry. Fireproof, storm-proof walls for generations.

Good questions
01Do I have the right soil?

Probably. About 70% of the world's soil makes good blocks. You need some clay and no organic topsoil, so dig at least 12" down.

  • Pure-earth blocks: 20–35% clay
  • Cement-stabilized blocks: 15–25% clay, with more sand

Test before you commit. Our Small Block Press makes test blocks at real machine pressure.

02How are earth blocks produced?

Soil is mixed with water (5–7% moisture) and, for weatherproof blocks, about 8% cement or lime. The mix is then pressed at up to 1,900 PSI.

  • 300 to 480 blocks an hour per machine
  • A Mega Mixer keeps the mix consistent at full speed
  • An on-site AECT plant makes 5,000–8,000 blocks a week
03What do earth blocks cost?

CEB walls typically cost under $20 per square foot of home, installed. Walls are only about 15% of a home's total cost.

Finished stabilized blocks start around $2.75 each plus delivery, and making your own costs far less per block at volume.

04How do you build with them?

Like any masonry: story poles keep walls plumb, and a bond beam ties the top together.

  • Stabilized blocks work anywhere, even basements
  • Run electrical surface-mounted, in a double wall, or in a cut chase
  • Leave blocks exposed, or finish with lime, earthen or clay plaster

05Is training included?

Quick answers

Questions we hear every day

Will my dirt work?

Probably. You need some clay (about 15–35%) and no topsoil. Dig 12" down and test it. Our Small Block Press makes test blocks at real machine pressure.

How much do CEB walls cost?

Usually under $20 per square foot of home, installed. Walls are only about 15% of a home's total cost. Get your exact numbers →

Which machine do I need?

Your block size decides it. The planner picks the right one for you, or browse all machines.

Stabilized or un-stabilized blocks?

If the wall will see rain, choose stabilized (about 8% cement). Pure-earth blocks are cheaper but need a roof overhang or plaster to stay dry.

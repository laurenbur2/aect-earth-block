How it works

From dirt to walls in four steps

Tap a step to learn more

Step 1 of 4 · Dig

Do I have the right soil?

Probably. About 70% of the world's soil makes good blocks. You need some clay and no organic topsoil, so dig at least 12" down.

  • Pure-earth blocks: 20–35% clay
  • Cement-stabilized blocks: 15–25% clay, with more sand
Test it first

Our Small Block Press makes quarter-scale test blocks at real machine pressure. Every AECT kit also includes a soil analysis.

Step 2 of 4 · Press

How are earth blocks produced?

Soil is mixed with water (5–7% moisture) and, for weatherproof blocks, about 8% cement or lime. Then it's pressed at up to 1,900 PSI.

  • 300 to 480 blocks an hour per machine
  • A Mega Mixer keeps the mix consistent at full speed
  • An on-site AECT plant makes 5,000–8,000 blocks a week
Training included

Every kit comes with a full week of one-on-one training at your site with AECT President Ryan Runge, plus lifetime support.

Step 3 of 4 · Stack & cure

Stabilized or pure earth?

Stabilized blocks (about 8% cement or lime) are fully water resistant and cure on pallets before they go on the wall.

Pure-earth blocks cost less and can go on the wall straight from the machine, but they need deep roof overhangs or a plaster finish to stay dry.

Many builders use stabilized blocks outside and pure earth inside.

What it costs

CEB walls typically run under $20 per square foot of home, installed. Get your exact numbers →

Step 4 of 4 · Build

How do you build with them?

CEBs go up like other masonry: story poles keep walls plumb, and a bond beam ties the top together.

  • Stabilized blocks work anywhere, even basements
  • Run electrical surface-mounted, in a double wall, or in a cut chase
  • Leave blocks exposed, or finish with lime, earthen or clay plaster

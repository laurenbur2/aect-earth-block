The 60-second version

What is a compressed earth block?

Soil with some clay in it, pressed by a machine at up to 1,900 PSI into a dense, uniform masonry block. Add about 8% cement and it's fully water resistant.

You build with CEBs the same way you build with any masonry. The difference: the material comes from your own land (or close to it), the walls hold heat and cool like nothing else, and the finished building is fireproof, insect-proof and storm-proof.

Why build with earth

One material. No downside.

01Fire and storm proof

Solid earth doesn't burn. CEB walls stand up to tornadoes, hurricanes, insects and even bullets.

02Lower bills for life

High thermal mass keeps the inside cool in summer and warm in winter. Owners can save $100,000+ in energy over a home's lifetime.

03Comparable build cost

Walls are only about 15% of a home's cost. CEB walls run close to standard materials but perform far better.

04Made from your land

Dig 12 inches below the topsoil and you likely have your building material. Less trucking, less carbon.

05Natural and healthy

No glues, no off-gassing. Earth walls breathe and regulate indoor humidity.

06Simply beautiful

Leave the blocks exposed, or finish with lime, earthen or clay plaster. Every wall has the color of its place.

Will my dirt work?

Probably. About 70% of the earth's soil makes good blocks. Two rules: avoid organic material (dig at least 12" below the surface), and you need clay. For un-stabilized or lime-stabilized blocks aim for 20–35% clay. For cement-stabilized blocks, 15–25% clay with more sand. Test before you commit; our Small Block Press ($3,100) makes quarter-scale test blocks at real machine pressures.

How much does it cost to build CEB walls?

Typically under $20 per square foot of home, material and labor, installed. Walls are only about 15% of total build cost, so a CEB home ends up very close to a conventional home in your area. Run your own numbers →

Stabilized or un-stabilized: which do I need?

Un-stabilized blocks are pure earth. They can go on the wall right out of the machine, but need protection from weather (deep porch overhangs, or a lime, stucco or paint coating). Stabilized blocks are about 92% earth and 8% cement, lime or fly ash. They're fully water resistant but need to cure. Many homes use stabilized outside and un-stabilized inside.

Which machine should I buy?

It depends on block size and volume. Building 1–5 houses with 6"×12" blocks: the Impact 2001A. Need speed with the same block: the 2500HD. Big 10"×14" blocks, high volume: the 3500. In California or anywhere interlocking reinforced blocks are required: the BP714. Compare all machines →

Do I need a mixer?

To get full output from any machine, yes. Consistent moisture (5–7%) and stabilizer mixing is what makes great blocks. Our Mega Mixer handles 10 cubic yards an hour, enough to feed a 3500, two 2500s, three Impacts or four BP714s.

Can I build a basement or run electrical?

Yes to both. Cement-stabilized CEBs can be used anywhere other masonry is, including basements (with an exterior moisture barrier). For electrical, surface-mount conduit, use a double wall with a cavity, or grind a chase into the wall and plaster over it.
